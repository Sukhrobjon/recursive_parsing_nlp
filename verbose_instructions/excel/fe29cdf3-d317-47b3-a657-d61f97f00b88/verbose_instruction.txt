1. Click any single cell inside your source data range (for example, click cell A2 on sheet “SalesData” that contains a transaction Date).  
2. Press Ctrl + T to convert the raw data into an official Excel Table; in the “Create Table” dialog leave “My table has headers” checked, then click OK. (Note that converting to a Table lets the PivotTable auto-expand when new rows are added later.)  
3. Navigate to the Ribbon tab “Insert”, then click the “PivotTable” button located in the “Tables” group.  
4. In the “Create PivotTable” dialog that appears:  
   a. Verify that the Table/Range box shows something like “SalesData[#All]”.  
   b. Select the radio button “New Worksheet”.  
   c. Click OK. Excel switches to a new worksheet named “SheetX” (the exact number may vary) and displays an empty PivotTable frame plus the “PivotTable Fields” task pane on the right.  
5. Drag the field “Date” from the field list into the quadrant labeled “Rows”.  
6. Drag the field “Revenue” into the quadrant labeled “Values”. By default Excel displays “Sum of Revenue”; if you instead see “Count of Revenue”, click the small drop-down in the “Values” area, choose “Value Field Settings…”, select “Sum”, then click OK.  
7. Right-click any date entry in the Row Labels area of the PivotTable, choose “Group…”, check “Months” (leave “Years” checked as well if available for multi-year data), then click OK. Excel collapses the raw dates into Month buckets such as “Jan”, “Feb”, etc.  
8. Double-click the cell that currently reads “Sum of Revenue” in the top-left corner of the PivotTable, type “Total Revenue”, then press Enter to rename the value column header.  
9. Verify that each month now shows a single numeric total; quickly sanity-check by comparing one month’s subtotal against the original data to confirm accuracy.  
10. Click any cell inside the PivotTable (for example, cell B6 under “Total Revenue”).  
11. Navigate to Ribbon tab “Insert”, then in the “Charts” group click “Column or Bar Chart” → select the first option under 2-D Column (“Clustered Column”). Excel inserts a bar chart (vertical bars) on the same worksheet.  
12. Drag the newly created chart so that its upper-left corner sits a few rows below the PivotTable (this keeps the table visible for future refreshes).  
13. Click once on the Chart Title placeholder, type “Monthly Total Revenue” and press Enter.  
14. Click once on the legend and press Delete to remove it (the data series is self-explanatory).  
15. Right-click any bar, choose “Format Data Series…”, and under “Series Options” ensure “Gap Width” is around 150 % (optional—this makes bars slightly wider and easier to read).  
16. Press Ctrl + S to save the workbook, ensuring the PivotTable and chart are preserved.  
17. (Verification) Return to sheet “SalesData”, append a new test row with a recent date and a known revenue amount, then go back to the PivotTable sheet, right-click anywhere inside the PivotTable, choose “Refresh”, and confirm both the PivotTable numbers and the bar chart automatically update to include the new month if applicable.
