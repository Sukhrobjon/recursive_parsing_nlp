1. Click the New button in the Metabase top-right toolbar.  
2. Select Question from the dropdown that appears.  
3. In the Choose a data source modal, click the Sample Database entry, then pick the Accounts table. We now have the Accounts dataset loaded for querying.  
4. Click the Summarize button (blue pill) just above the result grid.  
5. Under the Pick the metric you want to see section, click Count of rows. This tells Metabase to aggregate the total number of records.  
6. To the right of the word by…, click pick a column to group by.  
7. Choose Source from the list so each slice will represent a different account source.  
8. Click the blue Visualize button in the upper-right corner. A default bar chart appears.  
9. Click the Visualization dropdown (currently showing Bar) and select Pie from the list.  
10. Click Pie once more to open the Pie Options side panel.  
11. In the Display section, locate Show percentages and click the On the chart radio option. Percent values now overlay each slice, making it easy to see proportions.  
12. Click Done at the bottom of the side panel to save the visualization settings.  
13. Confirm we see a pie chart whose legend lists each unique Source along with both count and percentage—this verifies our aggregation worked.  
14. Click the cloud-shaped Download full results icon in the lower-right corner of the visualization.  
15. In the Download menu, choose .png and press Download. Metabase saves a file named something like Accounts - Source pie ...png to your browser’s default download folder.  
16. Switch to your file manager or terminal and navigate to /home/user/Downloads (e.g., Run `cd /home/user/Downloads && ls` in a terminal).  
17. Locate the newly downloaded PNG file (use the timestamp if multiple files exist) and rename it to pie.png (e.g., `mv "Accounts - Source pie 2024-05-12.png" pie.png`).  
18. Run `ls -l pie.png` or visually verify in the folder that pie.png now exists; we’ve successfully created and renamed the pie-chart image.
