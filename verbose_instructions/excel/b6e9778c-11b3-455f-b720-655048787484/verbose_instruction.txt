1. Click any single cell inside your raw data range on Sheet1 (for example, click cell A1 if your header row starts there).  
2. Press Ctrl + A to highlight the entire contiguous block of data that contains the two required columns – Product and Revenue.  
3. Click the Insert tab on the Ribbon.  
4. Click the PivotTable button in the Tables group.  
5. Verify the Table/Range box now shows the correct address (for example, $A$1:$B$500).  
6. Select the radio button “New Worksheet” so the pivot table is created on a separate sheet.  
7. Click OK; Excel adds a new sheet named something like “Sheet2 (PivotTable1)” and opens the PivotTable Fields pane on the right.  
8. Drag the field “Product” from the top list box down into the Rows area of the layout section.  
9. Drag the field “Revenue” from the top list box down into the Values area. Excel automatically applies the aggregation “Sum of Revenue.”  
10. Confirm that the first column of the pivot (Row Labels) now lists every product once, and the second column shows the summed revenue values.  
11. Right-click any number inside the “Sum of Revenue” column, choose Number Format, pick Currency (or Number with 0 decimal places), and click OK to improve readability.  
12. Click any blank cell inside the pivot table body to keep the pivot activated (a gray border will appear).  
13. Click the PivotTable Analyze tab (called just Analyze in some versions) that appears in the Ribbon.  
14. Click the PivotChart button in the Tools group.  
15. In the Insert Chart dialog, click Pie on the left pane, then choose the first subtype “Pie” in the upper-left of the right pane.  
16. Click OK; Excel inserts a pie chart that is automatically linked to the pivot data.  
17. Click the chart once to make the Chart Design and Format tabs appear.  
18. Click Chart Design → Add Chart Element → Legend → Right. Excel moves the legend to the right side, listing all product names.  
19. Click Chart Design → Add Chart Element → Chart Title → None (optional, if you prefer no title).  
20. Click anywhere outside the chart to deselect it; confirm visually that each colored slice corresponds to a product and the legend on the right lists those products.  
21. Press Ctrl + S to save your workbook, choosing a meaningful file name such as “ProductRevenueSummary.xlsx.”  
22. Double-check: change a revenue figure in Sheet1 and refresh the pivot table by right-clicking inside the pivot and choosing Refresh; verify that both the pivot totals and the pie slices update—this confirms the entire setup works correctly.
