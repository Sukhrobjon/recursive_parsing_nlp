1) Open your web browser and navigate to https://cloud.astronomer.io, then log in with your Astro Workspace credentials.  
2) Click the blue “Deployments” menu item in the left-hand navigation panel to view your list of Airflow deployments.  
3) Locate the deployment that should access your local PostgreSQL database, hover over its row, and click the “Open Airflow” button (small rocket icon). A new tab with the Airflow UI appears.  
4) In the Airflow UI, click the top menu labeled “Admin” and choose “Connections” from the dropdown.  
5) Click the blue “+ Add a new record” (or “+” button) in the upper right of the Connections list. A blank “Add Connection” form opens.  
6) Type postgres_conn in the “Conn Id” textbox so other DAGs can reference this connection via conn_id="postgres_conn".  
7) Click the “Conn Type” dropdown and select “Postgres” (or “PostgreSQL” depending on your version). This auto-adjusts the form to show Postgres-specific fields.  
8) Click the “Host” field and enter 172.17.0.1 to point Airflow at your local Docker/host network interface.  
9) Click the “Schema” (Database) field and enter jaffle_shop so Airflow targets the correct database.  
10) Click the “Login” field and type user (your PostgreSQL username).  
11) Click the “Password” field and type password.  
12) Click the “Port” field and type 5432, the default PostgreSQL port.  
13) (Optional) Scroll to “Extra” and leave it blank unless you need SSL or additional parameters; none are required for this local connection.  
14) Click the “Save” button at the bottom left of the modal. A green toast message “Successfully added” appears.  
15) Hover over the new row for postgres_conn and click the blue “Test” icon (plug symbol). Verify that the pop-up reads “Connection successful.”  
16) Close the Airflow tab and return to your Astro Deployment page. We’ve now created a reusable connection with ID postgres_conn that any DAG can reference.  
17) (Verification) Trigger or refresh any DAG that uses PostgresHook(conn_id="postgres_conn") and confirm tasks reach the “success” state, demonstrating that the Astro web UI connection to your local PostgreSQL database is functioning.
