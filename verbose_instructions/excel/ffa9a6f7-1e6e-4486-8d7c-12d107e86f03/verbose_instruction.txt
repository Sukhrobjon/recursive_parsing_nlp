1. Click cell A12 in Sheet1.  
2. Type “Total” and press ENTER so the label appears to the left of the totals row.  
3. Click cell B12.  
4. Type the formula `=SUM(B2:B11)` and press ENTER to calculate the total for the first month.  
5. Click cell B12 again; hover over its bottom-right corner until the small black “+” (Fill Handle) appears.  
6. Drag the Fill Handle horizontally from column B to column G, then release the mouse to auto-fill totals through G12.  
7. Verify totals: quickly compare one or two cells (e.g., B12) with the Status Bar “Sum” after selecting B2:B11 to ensure the numbers match.  
8. Click anywhere inside the data (for example cell B12) so Excel knows the range context.  
9. Click the Insert tab on the Ribbon.  
10. Click the “Bar Chart” icon in the Charts group, then choose the first 2-D Clustered Bar style. Excel inserts a temporary chart.  
11. With the chart selected, press Ctrl+1 (or right-click and choose “Format Data Range”) to open the Chart Design tools.  
12. Click “Select Data” on the Chart Design tab.  
13. In the “Chart data range” box, type:  
   `$Sheet1!$B$1:$G$1,$Sheet1!$B$12:$G$12`  
   and click OK — this forces the chart to use the header row (months) and the Total row.  
14. Click “Switch Row/Column” if necessary until you see one bar per month (not one long bar).  
15. Click “Add Chart Element” → “Chart Title” → “Above Chart”.  
16. Click directly on the title placeholder, type “Total”, and press ENTER.  
17. Click the chart once to show the plus-sign Chart Elements button; click the plus sign and uncheck “Legend” to remove the legend.  
18. Click an empty cell to finish the first chart.  
19. Click cell A13.  
20. Type “Growth” and press ENTER to label the next calculation row.  
21. Click cell C13 (Growth for the second month – the first month has no prior period).  
22. Type the formula `=(C12-B12)/B12` and press ENTER to compute decimal growth between months 1 and 2.  
23. Select cell C13 again, drag the Fill Handle across to column G, then release so all growth rates C13:G13 populate.  
24. Select growth cells C13:G13, then on the Home tab choose the “Percent Style” button if you prefer percentages; Excel automatically keeps the underlying decimal values.  
25. Spot-check one growth value: pick D13 and mentally confirm `(D12-C12)/C12` equals the displayed rate — this validates the formula copy.  
26. Click any cell within the growth row (for example C13).  
27. Go back to the Insert tab.  
28. Click the “Line Chart” icon, then choose the first 2-D Line option — a new chart appears.  
29. Click “Select Data” on the new chart’s Chart Design tab.  
30. Replace the “Chart data range” text with:  
   `$Sheet1!$C$1:$G$1,$Sheet1!$C$13:$G$13`  
   and click OK so the line references month headers and growth numbers.  
31. Confirm the horizontal axis now shows the correct months; click “Switch Row/Column” if it does not.  
32. Add a title: click “Add Chart Element” → “Chart Title” → “Above Chart”; type “Growth” and press ENTER.  
33. Remove the legend again: click the plus-sign Chart Elements button, uncheck “Legend”.  
34. Click outside the chart to deselect. Both visualizations should now be complete and placed on the worksheet.  
35. Press Ctrl+S (or click the floppy-disk Save icon) to save your work, ensuring totals, growth calculations, and both charts are preserved.
