1. Locate and open the workbook that already contains the raw expense data (assume it sits in Sheet1 with columns “Expense Account” in A and “Subtotal” in B).  
2. Click any single cell inside the data range (e.g., A2) so Excel can automatically detect the entire block.  
3. Press Ctrl + T to convert the range to an official Excel Table.  
4. In the “Create Table” dialog that appears, confirm the range (e.g., $A$1:$B$5000) and ensure the checkbox “My table has headers” is ticked, then click OK.  
   Note: Turning the list into a Table guarantees the PivotTable will dynamically pick up any new rows we add later.  
5. Click anywhere in the new Table to activate the contextual “Table Design” tab; we will use this later if we need to rename the table, but for now continue.

6. Navigate to the Ribbon tab “Insert” and click the button “PivotTable” (first icon in the PivotTable group).  
7. In the “Create PivotTable” dialog:  
   a. Verify the Table/Range field shows something like “Table1”.  
   b. Select the radio button “New Worksheet.”  
   c. Hit OK. Excel adds a new sheet automatically named “Sheet2” (or “PivotTable1”).  
   We now see a blank PivotTable placeholder on the left and the “PivotTable Fields” pane on the right.

8. Drag the field “Expense Account” from the top list into the area labeled “Rows.”  
9. Drag the field “Subtotal” into the area labeled “Values.”  
   Excel should immediately display “Sum of Subtotal.” If it shows “Count” instead, do the next sub-step, otherwise skip it:  
      a. Click the small drop-down beside “Count of Subtotal” in the “Values” box, choose “Value Field Settings…,” select “Sum,” and press OK.

10. Verify the PivotTable now lists every unique Expense Account down the left column and a grand total dollar amount in the right column.  
   Check one or two numbers manually against the source data (e.g., use SUMIF) to confirm the aggregation is correct.

11. With any cell still active inside the PivotTable, move to the Ribbon’s “PivotTable Analyze” tab, then click “PivotChart” in the “Tools” group.  
12. In the “Insert Chart” dialog, choose “Bar” on the left, then select the variant “Clustered Bar” in the gallery, and click OK.  
   Excel inserts a PivotChart right on the same worksheet.

13. Click an empty area inside the new chart to ensure it is selected, then:  
   a. Click the contextual “Chart Design” tab → “Move Chart.”  
   b. In the “Move Chart” dialog pick “New sheet” and type the name “Expense Chart” before pressing OK.  
   Now the bar chart sits on its own sheet, making it easy to present.

14. Double-click the default chart title “Sum of Subtotal” and type “Total Subtotal by Expense Account,” then press Enter.  
15. (Optional) Click the legend once and press Delete if you prefer to remove it—since each bar already has its account name, the legend is redundant.

16. Switch back to the PivotTable sheet by clicking its tab (“Sheet2”) to confirm the chart and table remain linked.  
   Change any subtotal directly in Sheet1 to test: the PivotTable → PivotChart should update as soon as you right-click the PivotTable and choose “Refresh.”

17. Press Ctrl + S to save the workbook so that the PivotTable and bar chart persist.
