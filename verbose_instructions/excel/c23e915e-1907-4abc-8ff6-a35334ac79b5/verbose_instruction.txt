1) Click any single data cell inside the original data range (e.g., A1) so Excel can automatically detect the entire block A:G.  
2) Press Ctrl + A to confirm the whole range A1:G(last row) is highlighted; this gives the PivotTable wizard the correct source.  
3) Click the Ribbon tab “Insert”.  
4) Click the green “PivotTable” button in the Tables group.  
5) In the “Create PivotTable” dialog, keep “Select a table or range” selected; verify the Table/Range field shows something like “$A$1:$G$5000”.  
6) Click the radio button “New Worksheet” so the pivot will live on its own sheet.  
7) Click OK; Excel inserts a new sheet named “SheetX (PivotTable1)” and shows the PivotTable Field List on the right.  
8) Drag the field “Product” from the top section down into the “Rows” quadrant of the Field List. We now see each unique product listed down the first pivot column.  
9) Drag the field “Revenue” into the “Values” quadrant. Excel automatically names this field “Sum of Revenue” and displays one number per product.  
10) Glance at the pivot values; if you see “Count of Revenue” instead of “Sum of Revenue”, right–click any number inside the pivot, choose “Summarize Values By”, and click “Sum”.  
11) Verify the Grand Total at the bottom equals the original revenue column total (quick check: use =SUM(G:G) in the source sheet and compare).  
12) Click anywhere inside the pivot so the PivotTable Analyze tab becomes active in the Ribbon (PivotTable Tools group).  
13) Click the little paint-roller icon “PivotTable Styles” if you wish to pick a style; choose “Pivot Style Medium 9” so totals are bold (optional aesthetic step).  
14) Click cell A3 on the pivot (first product name) just to ensure Excel knows the pivot is the active object—this affects the upcoming chart.  
15) While still on the PivotTable Analyze tab, click the “PivotChart” button in the Tools group.  
16) In the “Insert Chart” dialog, choose “Pie” from the list on the left.  
17) Select the first 2-D Pie subtype (top-left icon).  
18) Click OK; Excel inserts a default pie chart to the same worksheet.  
19) Drag the chart to a clear area below or to the right of the pivot so both objects are visible.  
20) With the chart selected, click the plus (+) icon that appears at the upper-right corner of the chart (Chart Elements button).  
21) Hover over “Legend”, then click the black arrow that appears, and choose “Right” so product names appear on the right.  
22) Still in Chart Elements, uncheck “Chart Title” if you prefer a cleaner look (optional).  
23) Click the colorful paintbrush (Chart Styles) and pick a color palette you like; Excel automatically maps each product to a slice color.  
24) Click any slice → press F2 → type a more descriptive label if needed (e.g., rename “ProdA” to “Product A”) and press Enter—this only updates chart labels, not pivot data.  
25) Hover over each slice; confirm the screen-tip shows the correct product name and revenue value—this cross-checks the summarization.  
26) Right-click a blank spot inside the chart area → choose “Format Chart Area” → in the Format pane, set “Fill” to “No Fill” if you want a transparent background (design preference).  
27) Press Ctrl + S.  
28) In the “Save As” dialog, navigate to your folder, type a file name like “Product_Revenue_Summary.xlsx”.  
29) Ensure the “Save as type:” drop-down shows “Excel Workbook (*.xlsx)”; this corresponds to Excel 2007-365.  
30) Click Save. The workbook now contains a pivot table summarizing revenue per product on its own sheet and a pie chart with legend on the right reflecting the same data.
