1. Right-click any sheet tab at the bottom, choose “Insert”, select “Worksheet”, and click OK.  
2. Double-click the new sheet tab, type Demographics, and press ENTER to rename it.  
3. Click cell A1.  
4. Drag across to cell C1 (so A1:C1 are highlighted).  
5. On the Home ribbon, click the “Merge & Center” button to combine the three cells.  
6. Type Demographic Profile in the merged cell and press ENTER.  
7. With the merged cell still selected, click the Fill Color dropdown (paint-bucket icon) on the Home ribbon, choose “More Colors…”, enter the hex value 0000FF, click OK – the background turns blue.  
8. Click the Font Color dropdown (A icon), pick White, then click the Bold ⌘B/CTRL+B button.  
9. Still in the merged cell, click the Center alignment button and then the Middle Align button so the title sits perfectly centered.

10. Click anywhere inside your source data table on Sheet1 (the sheet that contains the fields Sex, Civil Status, and Highest Educational Attainment).  
11. Press CTRL+A to ensure the whole data block is selected (first quick verification: the marching-ants border should frame all records).  
12. Go to the Insert ribbon and click “PivotTable”.  
13. In the “Create PivotTable” dialog, leave “Select a table or range” as is and tick “New Worksheet” OFF by selecting “Existing Worksheet”.  
14. Click inside the “Location” box, switch to the Demographics sheet, click cell A3, and press OK – a blank PivotTable frame appears starting at A3.

15. In the PivotTable Fields pane, drag Sex to the Rows box.  
16. Drag Sex again to the Values box – Excel shows “Count of Sex”.  
17. In the Values box, click the dropdown beside “Count of Sex”, choose “Value Field Settings…”, select the tab “Show Values As”, pick “% of Column Total”, and click OK.  
18. Click once inside cell A3 of the pivot to activate it, then type Percent by Sex and press ENTER – Excel automatically places this as a custom pivot caption.

19. Click any empty cell on the sheet to exit the pivot.  
20. Check that the percentages in column B sum to 100 % (quick verification step).

21. Note the last row number of this first pivot (it will usually finish by row 6 or 7 depending on categories). Click the first completely blank row after the pivot and leave it untouched as a spacer (e.g., if the first pivot ends on row 7, row 8 stays blank).

22. Click a convenient empty cell (e.g., A9) one blank row below the first pivot.  
23. Insert → PivotTable again; in the dialog leave the same data range, choose “Existing Worksheet”, click into “Location”, pick A9, press OK.

24. In PivotTable Fields, drag Civil Status to Rows.  
25. Drag Civil Status again to Values.  
26. Change the Values summarization just like before: dropdown → “Value Field Settings…” → Show Values As → “% of Column Total” → OK.  
27. Click cell A9 and type Percent by Civil Status, then press ENTER.  
28. Verify the displayed percentages add up to 100 %.

29. Leave the first blank row after this second pivot (for example, if the second pivot ends on row 14, keep row 15 empty).

30. Click cell A16 (or the first row after that blank spacer) to begin the third pivot.  
31. Insert → PivotTable → choose “Existing Worksheet”, set Location to A16, press OK.

32. In PivotTable Fields, drag Highest Educational Attainment to Rows.  
33. Drag Highest Educational Attainment again to Values.  
34. Configure Values to “% of Column Total”, then OK.  
35. Click cell A16 and type Percent by Highest Educational Attainment, press ENTER.  
36. Confirm the percentage column totals 100 %.

37. Press CTRL+S to save the workbook.

38. Final visual check: The Demographics sheet should read:  
   • Row 1: blue merged title with bold white text.  
   • Row 3: first pivot (Sex)  
   • One blank row gap  
   • Second pivot (Civil Status)  
   • One blank row gap  
   • Third pivot (Educational Attainment)  
   All pivot tables show percentages instead of raw counts and each caps at 100 %.
