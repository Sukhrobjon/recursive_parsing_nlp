1) Switch to the browser tab where Airbyte is running at http://localhost:8000  
2) Click the left-hand menu item “Sources” (database icon)  
3) Click the blue “+ New source” button in the upper-right corner  
4) Type “Postgres” into the modal’s search bar and click the card labelled “Postgres”  

5) Fill out the Postgres source form exactly as stored in desktop config.json (replace with your real values):  
```
Host:           127.0.0.1
Port:           5432
Database:       obesity_db
Schema:         public
Username:       airbyte
Password:       ********
SSL mode:       disable          # or "require" if your DB enforces TLS
```
6) Scroll to the “Replication Method” section, select radio button “Scan changes with user-defined cursor (CDC)” (this is what Airbyte labels as “Incremental | user-defined cursor”)  
7) Click the blue “Set up source” button at the bottom  
8) Wait until the green banner “All tests passed” appears – this confirms the connector can reach Postgres  

9) Click “Destinations” in the left menu  
10) Click “+ New destination” (upper-right)  
11) Type “Local SQLite” into the search bar and click the “Local SQLite” card  

12) In the destination form enter exactly:  
```
Destination Path: /local/obesity.sqlite
```
(Leave “Journal Mode” and “Vacuum” at their defaults)  

13) Click “Set up destination”  
14) Wait for the green “All tests passed” banner to verify the SQLite file is writable  

15) Click “Connections” in the left menu  
16) Click the blue “+ New connection” button  

17) In the “Define source” step click “Select existing source”, then choose the Postgres source you just created (it will be named the same as the database unless you changed it)  
18) Click “Next” to move to the destination step  
19) Click “Select existing destination”, then pick “Local SQLite”  

20) After Airbyte finishes loading available streams, locate the stream table (usually shows one row per table). Find the row for the table you want, e.g., “connection_file”  

21) Click the pill in that row under the “Sync mode” column (it defaults to “Full refresh | overwrite”)  
22) In the pop-up choose “Incremental | Append” from the drop-down list  
23) Notice the warning “Cursor field missing” turns red on the right; click that red text  

24) In the “Cursor field” modal turn on the radio button for column “id” (this sets id as the cursor)  
25) Click “Confirm” in the modal’s bottom-right corner to close it  

26) Verify back in the stream list that the “Cursor” column now displays “id” and the “Sync mode” column shows “Incremental | Append”  

27) Scroll to the bottom of the page and change “Frequency” to “Manual” (so we can test immediately)  

28) Click the blue “Set up connection” button (bottom-right)  
29) Wait until the status page loads and shows “Active” with a green check  

30) Click “Sync now” to trigger the first incremental append run  
31) Watch the log window; confirm it finishes with the green badge “Sync completed”  

32) Switch to your terminal and run:
```
sqlite3 /local/obesity.sqlite "SELECT count(*) FROM connection_file;"
```
33) Confirm that rows now exist, proving the data was written via the incremental append sync.
