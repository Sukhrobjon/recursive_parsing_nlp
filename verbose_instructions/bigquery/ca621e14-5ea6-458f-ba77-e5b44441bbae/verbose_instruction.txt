1) Double-click the folder named “Spider001” in Google Drive so we are inside that directory  
2) Right-click (or click the three vertical dots) on the file “a2012.xlsx” to open the context menu  
3) Hover over “Open with” without clicking so the sub-menu appears  
4) Click “Google Sheets” in the sub-menu; the spreadsheet opens in a new browser tab  
5) Click the top menu “Data” in the Google Sheets toolbar  
6) Click the item “Data connectors” in the drop-down  
7) Click “Connect to BigQuery”, then click the blue button “Get connected” in the side panel that opens  
8) In the “Add data connection” dialog, click the listed project name “bigquery-project”; this expands its datasets  
9) Click the dataset “customer_orders” so that its tables are shown on the right panel  
10) Click the table “orders”; we can see a green checkmark indicating it is selected  
11) Click the blue “Connect” button at the bottom-right of the dialog; a confirmation pop-up appears telling us the connection succeeded  
12) Click the “×” icon (top-right of the pop-up) to close this confirmation window; the data connector panel now shows “Connected”  
13) Click the blue “Share” button in the upper-right corner of the Google Sheets interface  
14) Click the drop-down that currently reads “Restricted” to open the link-sharing options  
15) Click “Anyone with the link” so the access level switches from restricted to public; Google shows “Viewer” as the default role  
16) Click the blue “Done” button to save the new permissions; the sharing dialog closes  
17) Click the browser tab title “a2012.xlsx” to verify we are back on the sheet; the BigQuery-connected rows will populate after a moment and the link is now publicly accessible
