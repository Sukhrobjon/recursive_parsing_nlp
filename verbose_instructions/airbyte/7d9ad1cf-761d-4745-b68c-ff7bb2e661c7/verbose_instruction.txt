1) Open your browser and navigate to http://localhost:8000 to reach the Airbyte local UI  
2) Click the blue button “Sources” on the left-hand navigation bar  
3) Click the “+ New source” button in the upper-right corner of the Sources page  
4) Type “Postgres” in the search bar that appears and click the tile “Postgres”  
5) Type a recognizable name in the “Name” field, e.g. `pg_orders_source`  
6) Fill in the Postgres connection details exactly as below (adjust if your credentials differ):  
   - Host: `localhost`  
   - Port: `5432`  
   - Database: `sample_db`  
   - Schema: `public`  
   - Username: `postgres`  
   - Password: `postgrespw`  
   - SSL mode: `disable`  
7) Click the “Set up source” button; wait for “Source test succeeded” to appear, then press “Continue”  
8) Switch to “Destinations” using the left-hand navigation bar  
9) Click the “+ New destination” button  
10) Type “SQLite” in the search bar and click the tile “SQLite”  
11) Enter a destination name, e.g. `local_sqlite_dest`  
12) Fill in the destination configuration:  
    - Destination JDBC URL: `jdbc:sqlite:/tmp/airbyte_sqlite.db`  
    (If the directory does not exist, Airbyte will create the database file on first sync.)  
13) Click “Set up destination”; after “Destination test succeeded” appears, press “Continue”  
14) Airbyte automatically opens the “Set up connection” page. Confirm that the “Source” dropdown shows `pg_orders_source` and the “Destination” dropdown shows `local_sqlite_dest`  
15) In the “Sync frequency” section, choose “Manual” (you can change this later)  
16) Scroll to the “Streams” table; locate the stream you want to replicate (for example `orders`)  
17) Click the small grey pencil icon to the far right of that stream row to open the “Stream configuration” side panel  
18) Click the “Sync mode” dropdown; select `Incremental | Append`  
19) Click the “Cursor field” dropdown; choose the field `id` (Airbyte will now track the largest id it has synced)  
20) Verify that “Primary key” stays empty (it is not required for Append mode)  
21) Click the blue “Apply” button at the bottom of the side panel; the side panel closes and the row now shows “Incremental | Append” under Sync mode and “id” under Cursor field  
22) Repeat steps 17-21 for any additional streams you want, always selecting `Incremental | Append` and cursor `id`  
23) Scroll to the bottom of the page and click the blue “Set up connection” button  
24) Wait for the connection overview page to appear, then click the “Sync now” button to trigger your first incremental append sync  
25) Watch the real-time logs; once the status changes to “Completed” with a green check, the data has landed in `/tmp/airbyte_sqlite.db`  
26) Switch to your terminal to verify the data landed correctly by running:  
```bash
sqlite3 /tmp/airbyte_sqlite.db "SELECT COUNT(*) FROM orders;"
```  
27) Confirm that the returned count matches (or is close to) the row count in your Postgres `orders` table; the incremental sync with cursor `id` is now fully operational
