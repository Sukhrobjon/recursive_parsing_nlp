1) Right-click the tab of the existing worksheet “Sheet1”, choose “Insert” → “Worksheet” so Excel adds a new sheet named “Sheet2 (2)”.
2) Double-click the new tab name, type “Sheet2”, and press ENTER to rename it exactly as requested.
3) Click cell A1 on Sheet2.
4) Hold SHIFT and click cell B1 so both A1 and B1 are selected.
5) Click the “Merge & Center” button on the Home ribbon (Alignment group). The two cells merge and a single wide cursor remains.
6) Type Summer sales ($) and press ENTER.  
   Note that the text is now centered across the merged cells; we will add the pivot starting in row 3, leaving row 2 blank for spacing.

7) Click any blank cell (for example A3) to deselect the header we just created.
8) Navigate to the “Insert” tab on the ribbon and click “PivotTable” (first icon in the Tables group).
9) In the Create PivotTable dialog, verify “Select a table or range” is chosen.
10) Click inside the “Table/Range” box, then switch to Sheet1 by clicking its tab.
11) Press CTRL+A (or CMD+A on Mac) to highlight the entire contiguous data block in Sheet1. The address (e.g., Sheet1!$A$1:$D$5000) appears in the selection box; we will use this range for the pivot.
12) Ensure “New Worksheet” is unchecked, enable “Existing Worksheet”, then click inside the “Location” box.
13) Click Sheet2’s tab. Click cell A3 so the location reads Sheet2!$A$3.
14) Press OK. Excel inserts a blank PivotTable starting at A3 and opens the PivotTable Fields pane on the right.

15) Drag the field that contains Month (e.g., “Order Month” or “Date”) to the “Rows” area.  
 Note: If the field is a full date, right-click any date inside the pivot, choose “Group…”, select only “Months”, and click OK so we see Jan, Feb, etc. as row labels.

16) Drag the field that contains “Sales Channel” (e.g., “Channel”) to the “Columns” area.
17) Drag the field that contains revenue values (e.g., “Revenue” or “Sales $”) to the “Values” area.  
 Excel shows “Sum of Revenue” by default; if it shows “Count”, click the drop-down in the Values area → “Value Field Settings…” → choose “Sum” → OK.

18) Confirm that the PivotTable now lists months down the left side and separate columns for each sales channel with summed revenue figures.

19) Click anywhere inside the PivotTable.
20) Navigate to the “PivotTable Analyze” tab, click the “PivotChart” button (Tools group).
21) In the “Insert Chart” window, select “Column” on the left, then choose “Stacked Column” (second icon) in the top-right preview area.
22) Press OK. Excel adds a stacked column chart, typically floating over the sheet.

23) Drag the chart so its top-left corner aligns roughly with cell A15 (below the pivot) to keep the sheet organized.
24) Click once on the chart title placeholder, type Revenue of Each Channel ($) and press ENTER.

25) Click anywhere on the chart to reveal the “Chart Design” and “Format” tabs.
26) Click the green “+” Chart Elements button at the chart’s top-right corner, hover over “Legend” and choose “Bottom”.  
 Verification: The legend now appears directly under the chart.

27) (Optional formatting) Click the chart border and use the handles to resize it so all months and channels are clearly visible.

28) Click an empty cell to deselect everything. Visually verify:  
 • Row 1 shows the merged header “Summer sales ($)”.  
 • Rows 3-? contain the pivot with months as rows and sales channels as columns, values summed.  
 • A stacked column chart titled “Revenue of Each Channel ($)” appears with legend at the bottom.

29) Press CTRL+S to save the workbook, ensuring all changes are stored.
