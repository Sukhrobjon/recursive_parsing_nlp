1. Click the sheet icon with a “+” (tooltip: New Sheet) located to the right of the “Sheet1” tab; Excel inserts a new worksheet named “Sheet2”.  
2. Double-click the tab label “Sheet2”, type Sheet2 (if not already), and press ENTER to confirm the sheet name.  
3. Drag to select cells A1:B1 on Sheet2.  
4. Right-click the highlighted range and choose Merge & Center (or Merge Cells, depending on Excel version).  
5. Type Summer sales ($) and press ENTER; the heading now spans columns A and B.  
6. Press CTRL+S to save your file so the new sheet and merged heading are stored.  
7. Click the Sheet1 tab at the bottom to switch back to the source data.  
8. Press CTRL+A to highlight the entire contiguous table of data (Excel surrounds it with the moving dashed border).  
9. Navigate to the Ribbon and Click Insert → PivotTable (first icon in the Tables group).  
10. In the Create PivotTable dialog, verify “Select a table or range” is chosen and the Table/Range box shows the correct address (for example Sheet1!$A$1:$D$500).  
11. Click the radio button Existing worksheet under Choose where you want the PivotTable report to be placed.  
12. Click the Location box, then click the Sheet2 tab and single-click cell A2; the box should now show Sheet2!$A$2.  
13. Click OK; Excel jumps to Sheet2 and inserts an empty PivotTable frame beginning at A2 with the PivotTable Fields pane open on the right.  
14. In the PivotTable Fields pane, drag the field named Product into the Rows area.  
15. Drag the field named Revenue into the Values area; Excel automatically displays “Sum of Revenue” next to each product row in the PivotTable at A3:B?.  
16. Confirm the PivotTable is correct by checking the Grand Total row (last row in the PivotTable) against the sum of the Revenue column in Sheet1 (optional quick check: in Sheet1, select the Revenue column and look at the Status Bar sum).  
17. Click anywhere inside the PivotTable range to keep it selected.  
18. While the PivotTable is still active, go to the Ribbon and Click Insert → Bar Chart → 2-D Bar → Clustered Bar (this produces a horizontal bar chart).  
19. Excel inserts the chart on Sheet2; with the chart still selected, press CTRL+X to cut it.  
20. Click a blank spot (e.g., A15) on Sheet2 and press CTRL+V to paste, placing the chart beneath the PivotTable for readability.  
21. In the Chart Tools → Design tab (or simply double-click the Chart Title), type Revenue of Each Product and press ENTER.  
22. With the chart still selected, click the plus (+) button that appears at the upper-right corner of the chart, uncheck Legend (or from Chart Elements list, uncheck the Legend box) so the legend disappears.  
23. Optionally, adjust bar colors or data labels if desired using the Format tab, but leave the default layout otherwise.  
24. Click outside the chart to deselect it and visually confirm:  
    • Row labels (products) appear on the vertical axis.  
    • Horizontal bars represent Sum of Revenue.  
    • Title reads Revenue of Each Product.  
    • No legend is visible.  
25. Press CTRL+S again and choose “Excel Workbook (*.xlsx)” or “Excel 2007–365” format in the Save dialog if prompted; click Save to preserve the pivot table, chart, and merged heading.
