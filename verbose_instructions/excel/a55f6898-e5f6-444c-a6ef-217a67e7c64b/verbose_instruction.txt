1. Click the “+” (Insert Sheet) icon immediately to the right of the “Sheet1” tab; Excel creates a new worksheet and automatically names it “Sheet2”.  
2. Double-click the newly created tab name, type “Sheet2”, and press ENTER to confirm, ensuring the tab is now titled exactly “Sheet2”.  
3. Select cell A1, hold SHIFT, and click cell B1 so that range A1:B1 is highlighted.  
4. Right-click anywhere inside the highlighted range and choose “Merge & Center” (or “Merge Cells” depending on your ribbon); the two cells are now a single merged cell.  
5. Type Summer sales ($) and press ENTER to store the header text in the merged cell.  
6. Click the “Sheet1” tab to switch back to your source data sheet.  
7. Press CTRL + A to highlight the entire contiguous data block (Excel should include the header row).  
8. Click the Insert tab on the Ribbon, then click PivotTable in the Tables group.  
9. Verify that “Select a table or range” is chosen and the Table/Range box correctly references your data (e.g., ‘Sheet1’!$A$1:$D$100). Leave “New Worksheet” selected for now and click OK; a blank Pivot Table Field List appears on a new sheet.  
10. Drag the Month field from the Field List into the Rows box; months now appear as row labels in the pivot table layout area.  
11. Drag the Sales Channel field into the Columns box; unique channel names now form column headers.  
12. Drag the Revenue (or Sales, depending on your header) field into the Values box; Excel automatically applies “Sum of Revenue”.  
13. Click anywhere inside the pivot table so the ribbon shows PivotTable Analyze.  
14. Click PivotTable Analyze → Move PivotTable → Existing Worksheet.  
15. In the dialog, click the Range selector (shrink icon), switch to Sheet2, and click cell A2 (just below your merged title). The reference box shows Sheet2!$A$2.  
16. Click the Range selector again to expand, then click OK. The pivot table instantly relocates to Sheet2 starting at A2.  
17. Scroll through Sheet2 and visually confirm that the first column lists months, the first row after row 1 lists channel headers, and the intersecting cells contain revenue totals—this verifies successful aggregation.  
18. Click any cell inside the pivot table, then press CTRL + A to select the entire pivot table (optional but makes chart creation easier).  
19. Click Insert on the Ribbon, choose the Insert Column or Bar Chart dropdown, and select Stacked Column (the second icon under 2-D Column). A default stacked column chart appears, usually overlapping the pivot table.  
20. Drag the chart to an empty region (e.g., columns G–L) so it does not obscure the pivot table.  
21. Double-click the generic Chart Title box; type Revenue of Each Channel ($) and press ENTER.  
22. Click anywhere inside the chart to activate Chart Tools, then click the plus icon (Chart Elements) → Legend → Bottom; the legend now sits below the chart.  
23. Optionally, right-click a blank region of the chart and choose “Move Chart…” if you prefer its own sheet; otherwise leave it embedded.  
24. Press CTRL + S to save the workbook, ensuring all modifications (title merge, pivot, and chart) are preserved.
