1) Click cell B2 to confirm the acceleration formula that is already present (we will replicate this formula downward in a moment).  
2) Move your cursor to the small square “fill-handle” located at the bottom-right corner of cell B2.  
3) Press and hold the left mouse button, drag the fill-handle straight down to the last populated row in your table (for example row 50), then release the mouse button. All cells B3:B50 are now filled with the same relative acceleration formula.  
4) Click any one of the newly filled cells in column B (e.g., B3) to verify that the cell references updated correctly (you should see row-relative references, such as “(A3-A2)” or similar, in the formula bar).  

5) Click cell D2 to select the second acceleration formula you have already created.  
6) Move to the fill-handle of cell D2, press and hold the left mouse button, drag it downward to the same last data row (e.g., row 50), and release the mouse button. Cells D3:D50 are now populated.  
7) Click cell D3 to confirm the formula references updated correctly for column D.  

8) Scroll to the top of the sheet again and click the first blank header cell immediately to the right of column D (this should be cell E1).  
9) Type the new header text: Combined Data, then press Enter.  

10) Click cell E2 to start constructing the concatenation formula that merges the values from columns A through D with their headers.  
11) Type the following formula exactly, ensuring you include the TEXT functions so all numeric values round to two decimal places:  
```excel
=CONCAT(
"A: ",TEXT(A2,"0.00"),", ",
"B: ",TEXT(B2,"0.00"),", ",
"C: ",TEXT(C2,"0.00"),", ",
"D: ",TEXT(D2,"0.00")
)
```  
12) Press Enter. You should now see a single line of text in cell E2 resembling something such as “A: 1.00, B: 0.25, C: 3.40, D: 0.25”.  

13) Click cell E2 again to select it.  
14) Locate the fill-handle at the bottom-right corner of E2.  
15) Press and hold the left mouse button, drag the fill-handle downward to the last occupied row (e.g., row 50), then release the mouse button. The concatenation formula is copied to every row in column E.  

16) Scroll to the bottom of the data to verify one of the newly filled cells in column E (e.g., E50). Check that:  
    • the text starts with “A: ” and ends with “D: ”,  
    • all four numeric values show exactly two decimal digits,  
    • commas and spaces match the pattern.  

17) (Optional) Switch to the Home tab, click the drop-down in the Number group, and choose “Text” for column E if you want to lock the appearance of the concatenated values (this prevents Excel from interpreting them as numbers or dates).  

18) Press Ctrl+S to save your workbook.  

19) Review the entire worksheet visually: columns B and D now contain acceleration formulas for all rows, and column E lists the rounded, header-annotated “Combined Data” strings.
