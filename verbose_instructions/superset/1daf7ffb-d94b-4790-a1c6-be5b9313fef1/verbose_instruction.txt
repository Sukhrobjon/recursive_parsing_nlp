1. Click any single cell inside the data range on sheet “flight” so Excel knows what area to use for the PivotTable.  
2. Press Ctrl + A to select the entire block of data (we should see a bold outline around every populated column such as Date, Department, Travel Class, Cost, etc.).  
3. Click the “Insert” tab on the Ribbon.  
4. Click the first button “PivotTable” in the Tables group.  
5. In the “Create PivotTable” dialog:  
    a. Verify “Select a table or range” is chosen and the Table/Range box shows something like ‘flight!$A$1:$D$5000’ covering all rows.  
    b. Select “New Worksheet” so the pivot goes to its own sheet.  
    c. Click OK. Excel inserts a blank pivot and opens the PivotTable Fields panel at the right.  
6. Drag the field “Cost” into the Values area. (Excel displays “Sum of Cost” by default; if you see “Count of Cost”, click the “i” / “Σ Values Settings”, choose “Sum”, and click OK.)  
7. Drag “Department” into the COLUMNS area.  
8. Drag “Travel Class” into the COLUMNS area and drop it just to the right of “Department” (Excel will nest it automatically so Department ➜ Travel Class).  
9. Drag “Date” into the COLUMNS area as well; drop it beneath Travel Class so it becomes the third-level column field.  
10. Right-click any date label that appears in the pivot header, choose “Group…”.  
11. In the Grouping dialog box:  
    a. Check only “Months”; uncheck “Years”, “Quarters”, and “Days”.  
    b. Click OK. Now the third-level expands to show “Jan”, “Feb”, … “Dec”.  
12. Remove any extra “Years” or “Quarters” fields that Excel may have added automatically: in the PivotTable Fields list, drag them out of the COLUMNS area until a small × icon appears, then release. We should now see exactly three column levels: Department (outer), Travel Class (middle), Months (inner).  
13. Drag the field “Date” a second time—this time into the FILTERS area (top of the field list). This will let us restrict the date range.  
14. Above the pivot, click the new Date filter drop-down (it shows “(All)” initially).  
15. Point to “Date Filters” ➜ click “Between…”.  
16. In the “Between” dialog, type these exact values:  
    • From: 1/1/2011 00:00:00  
    • To  : 6/30/2011 00:00:00  
    Click OK. The pivot instantly recalculates to include only costs inside that window.  
17. Verify we see months Jan through Jun only (Jul–Dec should disappear). This confirms the filter is working.  
18. Click any cell inside the pivot. On the Ribbon, switch to the “PivotTable Analyze” (or “Options” in Excel 2010) tab.  
19. In the very left text box labeled “PivotTable Name”, type TravelPrivot and press Enter. (Excel now refers to this pivot by that name.)  
20. Press Ctrl + S to save the workbook so the new “TravelPrivot” pivot table and its settings are preserved.
