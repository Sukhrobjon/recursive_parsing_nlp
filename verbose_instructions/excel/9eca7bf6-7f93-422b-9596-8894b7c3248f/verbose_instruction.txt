1) Click the plus-icon tab just to the right of “Sheet1” at the bottom of the workbook to create a new sheet named “Sheet2”.  
2) Press F2, type “Sheet2”, and hit Enter so the sheet is renamed (if it was not automatically).  
3) Click the “Sheet1” tab to return to the original data sheet where the Sales and COGS table lives.  
4) Drag to highlight cells A1:C11 (the header row plus the 10 weeks of numbers).  
5) Press Ctrl + C to copy the selection (Excel will outline the range with marching ants).  
6) Click the “Sheet2” tab to move back to the blank sheet where you want the chart.  
7) Click cell A1 on Sheet2 so Excel knows where to anchor the upcoming chart.  
8) Navigate to the Ribbon, click the “Insert” tab, then click the “Column or Bar Chart” icon in the Charts group.  
9) In the drop-down gallery, click the first option under “2-D Column” labeled “Clustered Column”. Excel will drop a default clustered column chart in the middle of Sheet2.  
10) While the chart is still selected, press Ctrl + V (or choose Home → Paste) to replace the placeholder data with the copied Sales & COGS range; you will see two colored series (one for Sales, one for COGS) and 10 category labels for the weeks.  
11) Click once on the chart title placeholder that currently says “Chart Title”.  
12) Type Sales & COGS, then hit Enter; the chart title now displays exactly “Sales & COGS”.  
13) Verify that the legend shows both series names (“Sales” and “COGS”) and that the horizontal axis lists Week 1 through Week 10; if anything is missing, press Ctrl + Z to undo and repeat steps 4–12.  
14) Click an empty cell outside the chart to finalize the selection.  
15) Press Ctrl + S to save the workbook; if this is the first save, the Save As dialog will appear—type a file name like “Weekly_Sales_COGS.xlsx” and click Save.
