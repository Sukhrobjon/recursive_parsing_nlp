1. Click any single cell inside your raw data table on sheet “SalesData” (e.g., A2 which should contain a date).  
2. Press Ctrl + A to highlight the entire contiguous data block so Excel knows the full range.  
3. Click the “Insert” tab on the Ribbon.  
4. Click the “PivotTable” button in the Tables group.  
5. In the Create PivotTable dialog:  
   a. Verify the Table/Range box shows something like `SalesData!$A$1:$B$5000` (adjusted to your data).  
   b. Select the radio option “New Worksheet”.  
   c. Click OK. Excel creates “Sheet1” (or “Sheet2” if that name is taken) with an empty pivot table frame.  
6. Locate the PivotTable Fields pane (usually on the right).  
7. Drag the field “Date” from the top list down into the “Rows” quadrant.  
8. Drag the field “Revenue” into the “Values” quadrant. It should default to “Sum of Revenue”. If it shows “Count”, click the dropdown arrow on that field → “Value Field Settings” → choose “Sum” → OK.  
9. Right-click any date label inside the pivot (e.g., “01/02/2024”).  
10. Choose “Group…”.  
11. In the Grouping dialog:  
    a. Click “Days” to deselect it (if selected).  
    b. Click “Weeks” so it is checked.  
    c. Ensure “Starting at” and “Ending at” look correct.  
    d. Click OK. The row labels now change to “2024-W01”, “2024-W02”, etc., each showing a summed revenue.  
12. Click once anywhere inside the pivot to activate the PivotTable Analyze ribbon.  
13. Press Ctrl + Space to select the entire pivot table body (not the Grand Total row).  
14. Click the “Insert” tab again.  
15. Click the “Line” drop-down arrow in the Charts group and choose the very first “Line” subtype. Excel drops a line chart on the same new sheet.  
16. With the chart still selected, go to the “Chart Design” ribbon and click “Add Chart Element” → “Chart Title” → “Above Chart”.  
17. Click inside the title box and type “Weekly Revenue”. Press Enter.  
18. While still on “Chart Design”, click “Legend” → “None” to remove the legend (redundant since we have only one series).  
19. Resize the chart: drag its bottom-right corner so it’s wide enough to show all week labels clearly.  
20. Verify the numbers: compare the Grand Total in the pivot table against a quick SUM in the raw data (e.g., on “SalesData”, in an empty cell type `=SUM(B:B)` and press Enter). Both totals should match.  
21. Save your work: press Ctrl + S, pick a filename such as “Weekly_Revenue_Report.xlsx”, and click Save.

We have now (1) summarized revenue by week in a pivot table on a new sheet, and (2) displayed that weekly revenue as a line chart on the same sheet.
