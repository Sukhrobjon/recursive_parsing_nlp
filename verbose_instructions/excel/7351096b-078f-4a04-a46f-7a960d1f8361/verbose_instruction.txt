1. Click the plus-shaped Add Sheet icon next to the last sheet tab at the bottom; Excel inserts a new worksheet named “Sheet2”.  
2. Click the Sheet2 tab so the sheet becomes active (the tab text turns white and bold).  
3. Click cell A1.  
4. Type Total Revenue and press Enter; Excel moves the cursor to cell A2.  
5. Click cell B1.  
6. Type Total Expenses and press Enter; Excel moves the cursor to cell B2.  
7. Click once on cell A2 (this will hold the revenue sum).  
8. Click in the Formula Bar directly above the column headers (the white input line).  
9. Type =SUM( but do not press Enter yet.  
10. Switch to Sheet1 by clicking its sheet tab.  
11. Drag-select the entire Revenue data range (for example cells A2:A20; include every numeric revenue value). Excel shows the marquee “marching ants” around the range and the formula now reads =SUM(Sheet1!A2:A20.  
12. Type ) to close the parentheses, then press Enter; Excel calculates the revenue sum and automatically returns you to the previously active cell—now Sheet1!A? is deselected.  
13. Switch back to Sheet2 by clicking its tab. Observe the computed Total Revenue value displayed in cell A2.  
14. Click cell B2 (this will hold the expenses sum).  
15. Click once in the Formula Bar.  
16. Type =SUM( and keep the cursor inside the parentheses.  
17. Switch to Sheet1 again.  
18. Drag-select the Total Expenses data range (for example cells B2:B20). You will see =SUM(Sheet1!B2:B20 in the bar.  
19. Type ) and press Enter; Excel records the sum for expenses.  
20. Return to Sheet2; confirm the value now appears in cell B2.  
21. Press Ctrl+S to save the workbook so the calculated totals are preserved.
