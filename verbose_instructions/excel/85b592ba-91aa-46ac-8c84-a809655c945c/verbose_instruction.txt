1. Select cell A1.  
2. Press Ctrl + Shift + End so the entire data block—from column A through column G and down to the last used row—is highlighted.  
3. Click the “Insert” tab on the Ribbon.  
4. Click the “PivotTable” button in the Tables group.  
5. In the “Create PivotTable” dialog, verify that “Select a table or range” is selected and that the Table/Range box shows something like `$A$1:$G$###` (### = last row).  
6. Choose the radio button “New Worksheet” (this places the pivot on its own sheet) and click OK.  
7. Switch to the new worksheet that just appeared (it will be named “SheetX” or “Sheet1 (2)” depending on existing sheets).  
8. In the PivotTable Fields pane on the right, drag the field called “Month” into the “Rows” area.  
9. Drag the field called “Revenue” into the “Values” area.  
10. Confirm that “Sum of Revenue” now appears under Values; if it shows “Count of Revenue,” click the dropdown ➔ choose “Value Field Settings…” ➔ pick “Sum” ➔ OK.  
11. Notice the PivotTable now lists each month in column A and its total revenue in column B—we will chart this next.  
12. Click anywhere inside the PivotTable to keep it selected.  
13. Press Ctrl + A to highlight the entire PivotTable (months plus totals).  
14. Click the “Insert” tab on the Ribbon again.  
15. Click the “Recommended Charts” or “Insert Column or Bar Chart” button in the Charts group.  
16. Choose “Clustered Bar” under the Bar section (horizontal bars) and click OK/Finish.  
17. A bar chart appears on the same worksheet; make sure the chart is still selected (border handles visible).  
18. Click the “Chart Elements” plus-sign icon to the upper-right of the chart, uncheck “Legend” so the legend disappears—since months are already on the axis, we don’t need the legend.  
19. Double-click the generic chart title text box, type “Total Revenue by Month,” and press Enter.  
20. (Optional) Drag the chart to a clear spot on the sheet so it doesn’t overlap the PivotTable.  
21. Verify accuracy: compare the largest bar with the corresponding largest total in the PivotTable—values should match exactly.  
22. Press Ctrl + S to open the Save dialog if this workbook has not been saved before.  
23. In “Save as type,” choose “Excel Workbook (*.xlsx)” to ensure Excel 2007-365 compatibility.  
24. Type a file name such as “Monthly_Revenue_PivotChart.xlsx,” choose your folder, and click Save.  
25. We are done—the workbook now contains a dedicated sheet with a PivotTable summarizing monthly revenue and an accompanying bar chart visualizing the totals.
